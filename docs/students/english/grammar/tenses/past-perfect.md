# 5 · Past perfect (simple & progressive) <Badge type="tip" text="class 7–10" />

## Goals
- build the **past perfect**: *had* + **3rd form**
- show what happened **before** another action in the past
- build the **past perfect progressive**: *had been* + **-ing** – and say **how long** something went on **until then**

When you tell a story in the past, sometimes you need to go **even further back**. That’s the job of the past perfect: the **past before the past**.
*Das past perfect ist die Vorvergangenheit: Es war schon vorher passiert.*

## Where is it in the grid? {#grid}

<TenseGraphic name="grid" mark="past-perfect,past-perfect-progressive" caption="Past tense + perfect aspect: had (past!) + 3rd form. With progressive: had been + -ing." />

::: tip Present perfect → past perfect
It’s the same idea as the present perfect – but you look back from a point in the **past** (THEN) instead of from **now**:
*I **have** finished.* (looking back from now) → *I **had** finished.* (looking back from then)
:::

## Past perfect simple {#simple}

<TenseGraphic
  name="past-perfect"
  caption="Top: first the film started (past perfect), then Anna arrived (simple past). Bottom: Tom was waiting for an hour – until the bus came."
/>

### Form

**had** + **3rd form** – the same for all persons

| | example |
|---|---|
| ✅ positive | She **had** already **eaten**. – **They’d** (had) left. |
| ❌ negative | I **hadn’t** seen the film before. |
| ❓ question | **Had** you ever **been** there before? |

### When do you use it?

| use | example |
|---|---|
| something happened **before** another action in the past | When we **arrived**, the film **had** already **started**. |
| to explain **why** something was like this | Tom was sad because he **had lost** his dog. |
| in reported speech (backshift) | She said she **had seen** a fox. → [Reported speech](../reported-speech/#backshift) |

::: info Which happened first?
① *The film **had started**.* → **first** (past perfect)
② *We **arrived**.* → **later** (simple past)
→ *When we arrived, the film **had** already **started**.* – We missed the beginning!

Compare: *When we arrived, the film **started**.* → We arrived, and **then** the film started. We saw everything.
:::

### Signal words

| signal words | example |
|---|---|
| **before**, **after** | **After** she **had done** her homework, she went out. |
| **already**, **just**, **never … before** | I **had never seen** snow **before**. |
| **by the time**, **when** | **By the time** we got there, everybody **had gone** home. |
| **because** (reason) | He was tired **because** he **had worked** all day. |

## Past perfect progressive {#progressive}

**had been** + **verb-ing** – for an activity that went on **for some time until a point in the past**.

| | example |
|---|---|
| ✅ positive | Tom **had been waiting** for an hour when the bus finally came. |
| ❌ negative | I **hadn’t been sleeping** long when the alarm went off. |
| ❓ question | How long **had** you **been living** there when you moved? |

| use | example |
|---|---|
| **how long** something went on **before** a point in the past | We **had been walking** for three hours when we found the café. |
| a **visible result** in the past | Her eyes were red. She **had been crying**. |

::: tip simple or progressive?
**had + 3rd form** → the action is **finished**, often with a result or a number: *She **had written** three letters.*
**had been + -ing** → the **activity** and **how long**: *She **had been writing** letters all morning.*
:::

## Typical mistakes {#mistakes}

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ *When I arrived, they already left.* → ✓ When I arrived, they **had** already **left**.
✗ *I had went home.* → ✓ I **had gone** home. (3rd form!)
✗ *She was tired because she worked all day.* → ✓ She was tired because she **had worked** (or **had been working**) all day.
✗ *When I came home, my brother has eaten all the cake.* → ✓ When I came home, my brother **had eaten** all the cake. (looking back from **then**, not from now)
:::

## Check yourself {#check-yourself}

1. When I got to the station, the train ___ (already / leave).
2. She didn’t want to see the film because she ___ (see) it before.
3. After we ___ (finish) dinner, we watched TV.
4. They ___ (play) for two hours when it started to rain.
5. I was hungry because I ___ (not eat) anything all day.
6. His clothes were dirty. He ___ (work) in the garden.

::: details Solutions
1. When I got to the station, the train **had already left**.
2. She didn’t want to see the film because she **had seen** it before.
3. After we **had finished** dinner, we watched TV.
4. They **had been playing** for two hours when it started to rain.
5. I was hungry because I **hadn’t eaten** anything all day.
6. His clothes were dirty. He **had been working** in the garden.
:::
