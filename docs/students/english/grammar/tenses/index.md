# Tense & aspect <Badge type="tip" text="the big picture" />

## Goals
- understand the difference between **tense** (*when?*) and **aspect** (*how do I look at it?*)
- see all English verb forms in **one simple grid**
- know that the **future** is not a tense of its own – there are several **ways** to talk about it
- choose the right form with **3 questions**

At school you learn the English “tenses” one after the other: simple present, present progressive, simple past …
This page shows you how they all **fit together**. It’s much easier than 13 separate forms!
*Im Englischen gibt es nur zwei echte Zeitstufen (present und past). Dazu kommen verschiedene Blickwinkel (aspects). Zusammen ergeben sie alle „Zeitformen“.*

## 1 · Tense or aspect – what’s the difference? {#tense-or-aspect}

Every English verb form answers **two questions**:

| question | name | possibilities |
|---|---|---|
| ⏰ **When** is it? | **tense** | 🟦 **present** – 🟦 **past** |
| 👀 **How** do I look at it? | **aspect** | **simple** · 🟥 **progressive** · 🟩 **perfect** · 🟩🟥 **perfect progressive** |

<TenseGraphic
  name="aspects"
  caption="The same action, four different views: as a whole (simple), in the middle of it (progressive), looking back from now (perfect), or going on until now (perfect progressive)."
/>

The colours are the same on every page of this chapter:

| colour | what it shows | how you build it | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🟦 **blue** | **tense**: present or past | the first verb shows the time: *work / works / worked, am / was, have / had* | I **worked**. |
| 🟥 **pink** | **progressive** – in the middle of it | **be** + **verb-ing** | I **am working**. |
| 🟩 **green** | **perfect** – looking back | **have** + **3rd form** | I **have worked**. |
| 🟧 **orange** | **future** – ways to talk about it | **will** · **going to** · … | I **will work**. |

## 2 · The grid: 2 tenses × 4 aspects = 8 forms {#grid}

Put the two questions together and you get a grid. **Every** English verb form has its place in it.

<TenseGraphic
  name="grid"
  caption="Read across: the aspect. Read down: the tense. Present + perfect = present perfect (have worked). Past + progressive = past progressive (was working)."
/>

| | simple | progressive | perfect | perfect progressive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **present** | I **work** · she **works** | I **am working** | I **have worked** | I **have been working** |
| | [→ chapter 1](./present) | [→ chapter 1](./present) | [→ chapter 3](./present-perfect) | [→ chapter 6](./perfect-progressive) |
| **past** | I **worked** | I **was working** | I **had worked** | I **had been working** |
| | [→ chapter 2](./past) | [→ chapter 4](./past-progressive) | [→ chapter 5](./past-perfect) | [→ chapter 5](./past-perfect) |

::: tip The building kit
Only **three** building blocks – you can combine them:
- **tense** → the **first** verb is present or past: *am – was · have – had · work – worked*
- **progressive** → **be** + **-ing**: *am working, was working*
- **perfect** → **have** + **3rd form**: *have worked, had worked*
- **both** → **have been** + **-ing**: *have been working, had been working*
:::

## 3 · And the future? {#future}

English has **no future tense** of its own. Instead, there are several **ways to talk about the future**:
*will*, *going to*, the present progressive and the simple present.
*Es gibt im Englischen keine eigene Zeitstufe für die Zukunft – nur verschiedene Wege, über sie zu sprechen.*

- **will** → *It **will rain** tomorrow.*
- **going to** → ***I’m going to visit** Grandma.*
- **present progressive** → ***I’m meeting** Tom at 4.*
- **simple present** → *The train **leaves** at 8:15.*

With **will** you can even use the aspects: *will **be working**, will **have worked***. All about this: [chapter 7](./future).

## 4 · Three questions to find the right form {#three-questions}

::: tip Ask yourself
1. **When?** Now / in general → **present** · finished time in the past → **past** · later → a **future** form
2. **In the middle of it?** Is the action going on at that moment (or only for a short time)? → **progressive** (be + -ing)
3. **Looking back from a later point?** Is there a link to *now* (or to *then*)? → **perfect** (have + 3rd form)
:::

Example: *Look! It’s snowing!*
1. When? → now → **present**
2. In the middle of it? → yes → **progressive**
3. Looking back? → no
→ **present progressive**: *It **is snowing**.*

Example: *Tom is tired – he ___ (play) football for three hours.*
1. When? → it started in the past and goes on until **now** → **present**
2. In the middle of it? → yes, an activity that goes on → **progressive**
3. Looking back from now? → yes → **perfect**
→ **present perfect progressive**: *He **has been playing** football for three hours.*

## 5 · The order you learn them at school {#school-order}

Most English books in German schools (for example *Green Line* or *Access*) teach the forms in this order.
Your book can be a little different – that’s OK.

| | form | example | usually in class | chapter here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | simple present | I **play** | 5 | [1 · present](./present) |
| 2 | present progressive | I **am playing** | 5 | [1 · present](./present) |
| 3 | simple past | I **played** | 5–6 | [2 · simple past](./past) |
| 4 | *going to* future | **I’m going to** play | 6 | [7 · future](./future) |
| 5 | present perfect | I **have played** | 6 | [3 · present perfect](./present-perfect) |
| 6 | *will* future | I **will** play | 6 | [7 · future](./future) |
| 7 | past progressive | I **was playing** | 6–7 | [4 · past progressive](./past-progressive) |
| 8 | past perfect | I **had played** | 7–8 | [5 · past perfect](./past-perfect) |
| 9 | present perfect progressive | I **have been playing** | 7–9 | [6 · perfect progressive](./perfect-progressive) |
| 10 | past perfect progressive | I **had been playing** | 9–10 | [5 · past perfect](./past-perfect) |
| 11 | future progressive | I **will be playing** | 9–11 | [7 · future](./future) |
| 12 | future perfect | I **will have played** | 10–12 | [7 · future](./future) |
| 13 | future perfect progressive | I **will have been playing** | upper school | [7 · future](./future) |

::: info One grid instead of 13 forms
In school these look like 13 different “tenses”. But you now know the trick:
**simple present** and **present progressive** have the **same tense** (present) – only the **aspect** is different.
And *will* and *going to* are not tenses at all, but **ways to talk about the future**.
:::

## Everything at a glance {#everything-at-a-glance}

| form | how to build it | example | typical signal words |
|---|---|---|---|
| simple present | verb (+ **-s** for he/she/it) | She **plays** tennis. | always, often, every day, never |
| present progressive | **am/is/are** + **-ing** | She **is playing** now. | now, at the moment, Look!, Listen! |
| simple past | verb + **-ed** / 2nd form | She **played** yesterday. | yesterday, last week, ago, in 2019 |
| past progressive | **was/were** + **-ing** | She **was playing** when … | when, while, at 5 o’clock yesterday |
| present perfect | **have/has** + **3rd form** | She **has played** twice. | ever, never, just, already, yet, so far |
| past perfect | **had** + **3rd form** | She **had played** before … | before, after, already, by the time |
| present perfect progressive | **have/has been** + **-ing** | She **has been playing** for an hour. | for, since, all day, how long |
| past perfect progressive | **had been** + **-ing** | She **had been playing** for an hour when … | for, since, when |
| *will* future | **will** + verb | She **will play** tomorrow. | tomorrow, next week, I think, probably |
| *going to* future | **am/is/are going to** + verb | **She’s going to play** later. | plans, Look! |
| future progressive | **will be** + **-ing** | She **will be playing** at 10. | this time tomorrow, at 10 tomorrow |
| future perfect | **will have** + **3rd form** | She **will have played** by 6. | by Friday, by then |
