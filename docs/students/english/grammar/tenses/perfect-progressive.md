# 6 · Present perfect progressive <Badge type="tip" text="class 7–9" />

## Goals
- build the **present perfect progressive**: *have / has been* + **-ing**
- say **how long** an activity has been going on **until now**
- choose between **present perfect simple** (*I have written*) and **progressive** (*I have been writing*)

This form combines **both** aspects: **perfect** (looking back from now) + **progressive** (an activity that goes on).
It answers the question **How long …?**
*Diese Form verbindet beides: Etwas hat in der Vergangenheit begonnen und läuft bis jetzt – wie lange schon?*

## When do you use it? {#use}

<TenseGraphic
  name="perfect-progressive"
  caption="Top: started two hours ago and still going on now. Bottom: simple = the result, how many – progressive = the activity, how long."
/>

| use | example | Deutsch |
|---|---|---|
| an activity that started in the past and is **still going on** | **I’ve been studying** **for** two hours. | Ich lerne seit zwei Stunden. |
| **How long …?** | How long **have** you **been waiting**? | Wie lange wartest du schon? |
| an activity that has **just stopped** – you can **see the result** | You’re all wet! **Have** you **been running** in the rain? | Bist du im Regen gelaufen? |

::: tip German „seit“ + Präsens = English present perfect (progressive)
*Ich **warte** seit 20 Minuten.* → ✗ *I wait since 20 minutes.* → ✓ **I’ve been waiting** **for** 20 minutes.
German uses the present – English needs the **perfect**, because it links the past with **now**.
:::

## Form {#form}

**have / has** + **been** + **verb-ing**

| | I / you / we / they | he / she / it |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ positive | **I’ve been** working. | **She’s been** working. |
| ❌ negative | I **haven’t been** sleeping well. | He **hasn’t been** feeling well. |
| ❓ question | How long **have** you **been** learning English? | **Has** she **been** crying? |
| 💬 short answer | Yes, I **have**. / No, I **haven’t**. | Yes, she **has**. / No, she **hasn’t**. |

## simple or progressive? {#simple-or-progressive}

| present perfect **simple** | present perfect **progressive** |
|---|---|
| the **result** – what is **done** | the **activity** – what you **were doing** |
| **How many? How much?** | **How long?** |
| **I’ve written** three emails. | **I’ve been writing** emails all morning. |
| **She’s read** the whole book. | **She’s been reading** for two hours. |
| **Who’s eaten** my cake? (It’s gone!) | **Who’s been eating** my cake? (Some is missing.) |

::: warning State verbs: only simple
With state verbs (*know, like, be, have = own, want*) you use the **present perfect simple**, even for *how long*:
✗ *I’ve been knowing her for years.* → ✓ **I’ve known** her for years.
:::

## Signal words {#signal-words}

| signal words | example |
|---|---|
| **for** + length of time | **It’s been raining** **for** three days. |
| **since** + starting point | **We’ve been waiting** **since** 9 o’clock. |
| **How long …?** | **How long** have you been playing the guitar? |
| all day, all morning, the whole week | **I’ve been working** **all day**. |
| lately, recently | **I’ve been feeling** tired **lately**. |

## Typical mistakes {#mistakes}

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ *I wait here since ten minutes.* → ✓ **I’ve been waiting** here **for** ten minutes.
✗ *I am learning English since five years.* → ✓ **I’ve been learning** English **for** five years.
✗ *She has been writing three letters.* → ✓ She **has written** three letters. (a number → simple)
✗ *How long are you living here?* → ✓ How long **have** you **been living** here?
:::

## Check yourself {#check-yourself}

Present perfect simple or progressive?

1. I ___ (wait) for the bus for 20 minutes!
2. Tom ___ (eat) three bananas today.
3. How long ___ you ___ (learn) the piano?
4. Your hands are dirty. What ___ you ___ (do)?
5. I ___ (know) Lisa since primary school.
6. We ___ (paint) the kitchen all day, but we ___ (not finish) yet.

::: details Solutions
1. **I’ve been waiting** for the bus for 20 minutes!
2. Tom **has eaten** three bananas today. (how many → simple)
3. How long **have** you **been learning** the piano?
4. Your hands are dirty. What **have** you **been doing**? (visible result)
5. **I’ve known** Lisa since primary school. (state verb *know* → simple)
6. **We’ve been painting** the kitchen all day, but we **haven’t finished** yet.
:::
