# 7 · Talking about the future <Badge type="tip" text="class 6–12" />

## Goals
- know that English has **no future tense** – but **four main ways** to talk about the future
- choose between **will**, **going to**, the **present progressive** and the **simple present**
- understand the **future progressive** (*will be doing*) and the **future perfect** (*will have done*)

German speakers often use the present for the future: *Morgen **gehe** ich ins Kino.*
English is different: you choose a form that shows **what kind of future** you mean – a prediction? a plan? an appointment?
*Im Englischen gibt es keine eigene Zukunftszeit. Die Form zeigt, wie sicher oder wie geplant etwas ist.*

## Four ways to talk about the future {#four-ways}

### will {#will}

**will** + infinitive – the same for all persons. Short form: **’ll** · negative: **won’t**

| use | example |
|---|---|
| 🔮 **prediction** – what you think or know about the future | It **will be** sunny tomorrow. – I think **you’ll like** the film. |
| ⚡ a **decision** you make **now**, in the moment of speaking | The phone is ringing. – **I’ll get** it! |
| 🤝 **promises**, offers, requests | I **won’t tell** anybody. – **I’ll help** you. – **Will** you **open** the window, please? |

Signal words: *I think, I’m sure, probably, maybe, perhaps, tomorrow, next year, in 2050*

### going to {#going-to}

**am / is / are going to** + infinitive

| use | example |
|---|---|
| 📋 a **plan** or **intention** – you decided **before** | **I’m going to visit** my grandma at the weekend. |
| 👀 you can **see** it coming – there are signs **now** | Look at those black clouds! **It’s going to rain**. |

::: tip will or going to?
*– We haven’t got any milk.* *– Oh, **I’ll buy** some.* → decided **now** → **will**
*– Why are you taking your bag?* *– **I’m going to buy** some milk.* → decided **before** → **going to**
:::

### present progressive for arrangements {#present-progressive}

**am / is / are** + **-ing** – for **fixed arrangements** with other people, with a time and place. It’s in your diary 📅.

- **I’m meeting** Tom at the cinema at 7 tonight.
- **We’re flying** to Rome on Friday. (The tickets are booked.)
- What **are** you **doing** this weekend?

### simple present for timetables {#simple-present}

For **timetables**, **programmes** and fixed events – not because somebody wants it, but because it’s planned officially.

- The train **leaves** at 8:15.
- The film **starts** at 7 pm.
- School **ends** on 20 July.

::: warning After when, if, before, after …: no will!
In sentences with **when, if, before, after, as soon as, until** about the future, use the **simple present**:
✗ *When I will be 18, I will learn to drive.* → ✓ When I **am** 18, **I’ll learn** to drive.
✗ *I’ll call you as soon as I will arrive.* → ✓ I’ll call you as soon as I **arrive**.
:::

## Aspect in the future: future progressive and future perfect {#aspect}

With **will** you can use the same aspects as in the present and past.

<TenseGraphic
  name="future-aspect"
  caption="Future progressive: in the middle of an action at a time in the future. Future perfect: already finished before a time in the future."
/>

| form | how to build it | use | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **future progressive** | **will be** + **-ing** | an action **going on** at a time in the future | This time tomorrow **I’ll be sitting** on the plane. |
| **future perfect** | **will have** + **3rd form** | an action **finished before** a time in the future | **By** Friday **I’ll have finished** my project. |
| **future perfect progressive** | **will have been** + **-ing** | **how long** – until a time in the future | **By** June **I’ll have been living** here **for** ten years. |

::: info Do I have to use these forms?
The **future progressive** and the **future perfect** are useful, but you can often say the same thing with *will*.
The **future perfect progressive** is rare – it’s enough if you **understand** it when you read it.
Signal words: *this time tomorrow, at 10 tomorrow* (progressive) · *by Friday, by then, by the time* (perfect)
:::

## Typical mistakes {#mistakes}

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ *Tomorrow I go to the cinema with Tom.* → ✓ Tomorrow **I’m going** to the cinema with Tom. (arrangement)
✗ *I will to call you.* → ✓ I **will call** you. (no *to* after *will*)
✗ *Look! It will fall!* → ✓ Look! **It’s going to fall**! (you can see it coming)
✗ *If it will rain, we will stay at home.* → ✓ If it **rains**, **we’ll stay** at home.
✗ *What do you do this weekend?* → ✓ What **are** you **doing** this weekend? (asking about plans and arrangements)
:::

## Check yourself {#check-yourself}

Choose the best form: *will*, *going to*, present progressive or simple present.

1. Look at that ladder! It ___ (fall).
2. – I’m so thirsty. – Wait, I ___ (get) you some water.
3. The bus ___ (leave) at 7:45 tomorrow morning.
4. I ___ (meet) my friends at the park at 4 o’clock. We arranged it yesterday.
5. I think it ___ (be) a great party.
6. When I ___ (finish) school, I ___ (travel) around the world.

::: details Solutions
1. Look at that ladder! **It’s going to fall**. (you can see it coming)
2. Wait, **I’ll get** you some water. (decision now)
3. The bus **leaves** at 7:45 tomorrow morning. (timetable)
4. **I’m meeting** my friends at the park at 4 o’clock. (arrangement)
5. I think it **will be** a great party. (prediction)
6. When I **finish** school, **I’m going to travel** (or **’ll travel**) around the world. (*when* → simple present)
:::
