# 3 · Present perfect vs simple past <Badge type="tip" text="class 6" />

## Goals
- build the **present perfect**: *have / has* + **3rd form**
- use it for **experiences**, **results now** and things that **are still going on**
- decide: **present perfect** or **simple past**?

Both forms talk about the past. But they look at it in **different ways**:
the **simple past** looks at a **finished time** – the **present perfect** looks **back from now** and is interested in **now**.
*Simple past: Wann ist es passiert? – Present perfect: Was bedeutet es jetzt?*

## Where is it in the grid? {#grid}

<TenseGraphic name="grid" mark="present-perfect" caption="Present tense + perfect aspect: have/has (present!) + 3rd form. That’s why it is linked to now." />

::: tip Why “present” perfect?
Look at the first verb: **have / has** is **present**! That’s the link to **now**.
*Das present perfect ist eine Gegenwartsform, die zurückschaut.*
:::

## The big contrast {#contrast}

<TenseGraphic
  name="perfect-vs-past"
  caption="Simple past: one visit in a finished time (last year). Present perfect: three visits in my life until now – we don’t say when."
/>

| | simple past | present perfect |
|---|---|---|
| **question** | **When** did it happen? | What is the **result** / the **experience** **now**? |
| **time** | a **finished** time: yesterday, last year, in 2019 | **no** exact time, or a time that is **not finished**: ever, never, today, this week, so far |
| **example** | I **lost** my key **yesterday**. | I **have lost** my key. (= I don’t have it now.) |
| | I **visited** London **in 2022**. | I **have visited** London three times. |

## Form {#form}

**have / has** + **3rd form** (past participle)

| | I / you / we / they | he / she / it |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ positive | **I’ve** (have) finished. | **She’s** (has) finished. |
| ❌ negative | I **haven’t** finished. | She **hasn’t** finished. |
| ❓ question | **Have** you finished? | **Has** she finished? |
| 💬 short answer | Yes, I **have**. / No, I **haven’t**. | Yes, she **has**. / No, she **hasn’t**. |

The 3rd form: regular verbs end in **-ed** (*played, finished*). Irregular verbs have their own 3rd form:
*be – **been** · go – **gone** · see – **seen** · do – **done** · eat – **eaten** · write – **written** · take – **taken** · buy – **bought***

## When do you use it? {#use}

| use | example | signal words |
|---|---|---|
| 🌍 **experiences** in your life (until now) | **Have** you **ever been** to Spain? – **I’ve never eaten** sushi. | ever, never, before, so far, … times |
| ✅ a **result** you can see **now** | **I’ve lost** my key. (I can’t get in.) – **She’s cut** her finger. | just, already, yet |
| ⏳ something that **started** in the past and is **still true** | **We’ve lived** here **for** ten years. – **I’ve known** her **since** 2020. | for, since, how long |
| 📅 a time that is **not finished** | **I’ve drunk** three glasses of water **today**. | today, this week, this year |

### just, already, yet {#just-already-yet}

| word | meaning | position | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **just** | gerade eben | between *have* and the verb | **I’ve just** eaten. |
| **already** | schon | between *have* and the verb | **She’s already** finished. |
| **yet** | schon? / noch nicht | at the **end** of questions and negatives | **Have** you done it **yet**? – I **haven’t** done it **yet**. |

### for or since? {#for-since}

German *seit* has **two** English words:
- **for** + a **length of time**: *for ten years, for two hours, for a long time*
- **since** + a **starting point**: *since 2015, since Monday, since I was six*

*Ich wohne seit 10 Jahren hier.* → I **have lived** here **for** ten years.
More about *for* and *since*: [Prepositions of time](../prepositions/time#for-since-until-by-during).

## Typical mistakes {#mistakes}

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ *I have seen him yesterday.* → ✓ I **saw** him yesterday. (finished time → simple past)
✗ *I live here since 2015.* → ✓ I **have lived** here since 2015.
✗ *I know her for three years.* → ✓ I **have known** her for three years.
✗ *When have you arrived?* → ✓ When **did** you arrive? (*when* asks for a time → simple past)
✗ *Did you ever eat sushi?* → ✓ **Have** you **ever eaten** sushi? (experience)
:::

::: tip The test question
Can you add a **finished time** (yesterday, last year, in 2019, two days ago)? → **simple past**.
Is it about **now** – an experience, a result, or something still going on? → **present perfect**.
:::

## Check yourself {#check-yourself}

Present perfect or simple past?

1. I ___ (never / be) to the USA.
2. We ___ (go) to the zoo last Sunday.
3. Oh no! I ___ (forget) my sports bag. I can’t do PE today.
4. My parents ___ (know) each other since 2005.
5. When ___ you ___ (start) learning English?
6. ___ you ___ (finish) your homework yet?

::: details Solutions
1. **I’ve never been** to the USA. (experience)
2. We **went** to the zoo last Sunday. (finished time)
3. Oh no! **I’ve forgotten** my sports bag. (result now)
4. My parents **have known** each other since 2005. (still true)
5. When **did** you **start** learning English? (*when* → simple past)
6. **Have** you **finished** your homework yet? (*yet*)
:::
