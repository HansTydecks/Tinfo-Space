# Passive voice <Badge type="tip" text="the passive" />

## Goals
- turn an **active** sentence into a **passive** sentence
- build the passive with **be + past participle** in every tense
- decide if you need **by …** or not
- understand **advanced** forms: *I was given …*, *She had her car repaired*, *He is said to be …*
- use the spoken **got**-passive correctly: *My phone **got stolen**.* · *Did he **get caught**?*

In an **active** sentence we say **who does** something. In a **passive** sentence we say **what happens** to something or someone.
*Im Aktiv steht im Mittelpunkt, wer etwas tut. Im Passiv steht im Mittelpunkt, was mit etwas oder jemandem passiert.*

## What changes? The big picture {#big-picture}

<PassiveGraphic
  name="overview"
  caption="The object of the active sentence (the jewels) moves to the front and becomes the subject. The verb becomes be + past participle. The doer (someone) is often left out."
/>

The colours are the same in every picture on this page and in the game:

| colour | what it shows | example |
|---|---|---|
| 🟦 **blue** | the **object → new subject** – the thing something happens to | Someone stole **the jewels**. → **The jewels** were stolen. |
| 🟥 **pink** | the form of **be** – it shows the **tense** | **is** · **was** · **has been** · **will be** |
| 🟩 **green** | the **past participle** (3rd form) – it never changes | stolen · broken · opened |
| 🟧 **orange** | the **doer** (*by …*) and **word order** | … **by a tall man** · **It** is said that … |

::: tip The 4 questions for every sentence
1. **What is the object** of the active sentence? → it becomes the **new subject**
2. **Which tense** is the active verb in? → keep the **same tense**
3. **Form of be** in that tense – and does it fit the new subject? (*the window **was** / the windows **were***)
4. **+ past participle** (3rd form) – and do you need **by …**?
:::

## 1 · The form: be + past participle {#form}

<PassiveGraphic
  name="formula"
  caption="be shows the tense and agrees with the subject. The past participle (3rd form) never changes."
/>

| active | → | passive |
|---|:-:|---|
| The police **check** the cameras. | → | The cameras **are checked**. |
| The thief **opened** the safe. | → | The safe **was opened** (by the thief). |
| Someone **broke** the window. | → | The window **was broken**. |
| They **found** the bag in the river. | → | The bag **was found** in the river. |
| A dog **ate** the cake. | → | The cake **was eaten** by a dog. |

**Irregular verbs** – the 3rd form is the important one:

| base | past simple | **past participle** | | base | past simple | **past participle** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| steal | stole | **stolen** | | take | took | **taken** |
| break | broke | **broken** | | write | wrote | **written** |
| hide | hid | **hidden** | | see | saw | **seen** |
| find | found | **found** | | catch | caught | **caught** |
| leave | left | **left** | | eat | ate | **eaten** |
| give | gave | **given** | | show | showed | **shown** |

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ The jewels **were stole**. → ✓ The jewels **were stolen**. (3rd form, not past simple)
✗ The window **broken** last night. → ✓ The window **was broken** last night. (be is missing)
✗ The windows **was** broken. → ✓ The windows **were** broken. (be agrees with the new subject)
:::

## 2 · When do we use the passive? {#when}

<PassiveGraphic
  name="when"
  caption="The passive puts the action – or the thing it happens to – in the spotlight."
/>

- The doer is **unknown**: *My phone **was stolen** on the bus.*
- The doer is **obvious**: *The burglar **was arrested**.* (by the police – who else?)
- The **action** is more important than the doer – typical in **news** and **police reports**: *Two shops **were robbed** last night.*
- **Processes, facts, science, instructions**: *The fingerprints **are checked** in a lab.* · *Paper **was invented** in China.*

*Das Passiv benutzt man, wenn der Täter unbekannt, offensichtlich oder unwichtig ist – oder wenn die Handlung im Mittelpunkt steht.*

## 3 · The passive in all tenses {#tenses}

<PassiveGraphic
  name="tenses"
  caption="Use the same tense as in the active sentence. Only be changes – the past participle (broken) always stays the same."
/>

| tense | active | passive = **be** + **past participle** |
|---|---|---|
| **present simple** | They **check** the cameras. | The cameras **are checked**. |
| **present progressive** | They **are searching** the house. | The house **is being searched**. |
| **present perfect** | Someone **has stolen** my bike. | My bike **has been stolen**. |
| **past simple** | Someone **robbed** the bank. | The bank **was robbed**. |
| **past progressive** | They **were questioning** the guard. | The guard **was being questioned**. |
| **past perfect** | Someone **had opened** the safe. | The safe **had been opened**. |
| **will-future** | They **will catch** the thief. | The thief **will be caught**. |
| **going to** | They **are going to close** the road. | The road **is going to be closed**. |

::: tip Trick: being and been
**Progressive** tenses (*-ing*) → **being**: *is **being** searched*, *was **being** questioned*
**Perfect** tenses (*have / has / had*) → **been**: *has **been** stolen*, *had **been** opened*
:::

## 4 · Modal verbs {#modals}

<PassiveGraphic
  name="modals"
  caption="Modal verb + be + past participle. be never changes after a modal verb."
/>

| active | passive |
|---|---|
| We **can** open the safe. | The safe **can be opened**. |
| We **can’t** find the key. | The key **can’t be found**. |
| You **must** lock the door. | The door **must be locked**. |
| You **should** check the cameras. | The cameras **should be checked**. |
| Someone **might** see us. | We **might be seen**. |
| We **have to** question the witnesses. | The witnesses **have to be questioned**. |
| They **will** arrest the gang. | The gang **will be arrested**. |

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ The door **must been** locked. → ✓ The door **must be** locked.
✗ The safe **can opened**. → ✓ The safe **can be** opened.
:::

## 5 · The by-agent: by … or not? {#by-agent}

<PassiveGraphic
  name="by-agent"
  caption="Keep by … when it is new or important information. Leave it out when the doer is unknown, obvious or not important."
/>

| active | passive | why? |
|---|---|---|
| **A tall man** broke the window. | The window was broken **by a tall man**. | important clue → keep it |
| **A security camera** filmed the thief. | The thief was filmed **by a security camera**. | important → keep it |
| **Someone** stole my bike. | My bike was stolen. | unknown → leave it out |
| **They** are building a new police station. | A new police station is being built. | not important → leave it out |
| **People** speak English here. | English is spoken here. | everybody → leave it out |
| **The police** arrested the thief. | The thief was arrested. | obvious → usually left out |

::: warning by + object pronoun
If you need a pronoun after *by*, use the **object form**: by **me**, by **him**, by **her**, by **us**, by **them**.
✗ *by he*, ✗ *by they*
:::

## 6 · Negatives and questions {#questions}

<PassiveGraphic
  name="questions"
  caption="No do / does / did in the passive! Questions: be comes before the subject. Negatives: be + not."
/>

| | active | passive |
|---|---|---|
| **negative** | They **didn’t** lock the door. | The door **wasn’t** locked. |
| | Nobody **has** found the key. | The key **hasn’t been** found. |
| | They **don’t** check the cameras. | The cameras **aren’t** checked. |
| **yes / no question** | **Did** they find the bag? | **Was** the bag found? |
| | **Have** they caught the thief? | **Has** the thief **been** caught? |
| | **Will** they close the road? | **Will** the road **be** closed? |
| **wh-question** | Where **did** they hide the money? | Where **was** the money hidden? |
| | When **do** they lock the gate? | When **is** the gate locked? |

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ **Did** the bag **found**? → ✓ **Was** the bag found?
✗ The door **didn’t** locked. → ✓ The door **wasn’t** locked.
:::

## 7 · Verbs with two objects {#two-objects}

Some verbs have **two objects**: a **person** and a **thing** – *give, send, show, offer, tell, promise, hand, teach, pay*.

<PassiveGraphic
  name="two-objects"
  caption="Both objects can become the subject. With the thing first you need “to” before the person."
/>

| active | passive ① person first | passive ② thing first |
|---|---|---|
| Someone **gave** the guard a note. | **The guard** was given a note. | **A note** was given **to** the guard. |
| They **showed** me the photos. | **I** was shown the photos. | **The photos** were shown **to** me. |
| The bank **offered** her a reward. | **She** was offered a reward. | **A reward** was offered **to** her. |

::: tip Which one?
In English, **person first** is more common: ***I** was given a key.* – *Mir wurde ein Schlüssel gegeben.*
:::

## 8 · Advanced passive {#advanced}

<PassiveGraphic
  name="have-done"
  caption="have something done: someone does it for you. People say that … → He is said to … or It is said that …"
/>

### have / get something done
You don’t do it yourself – **someone does it for you** (often a service you pay for).

| active | have + object + past participle |
|---|---|
| A locksmith **changed** her locks. | She **had** her locks **changed**. |
| A mechanic **is repairing** our car. | We **are having** our car **repaired**. |
| A hairdresser **will cut** my hair. | I **will have** my hair **cut**. |
| Someone **stole** his phone. | He **had** his phone **stolen**. (something bad happened to him) |

In spoken English you can also say *get*: *I **got** my bike **fixed**.*

### He is said to … / It is said that …
With verbs like **say, think, believe, know, report, expect** there are **two** passive forms:

| active | ① It + passive + that … | ② subject + passive + to … |
|---|---|---|
| People **say** that he is rich. | **It is said that** he is rich. | He **is said to be** rich. |
| People **believe** that the gang lives in London. | **It is believed that** the gang lives in London. | The gang **is believed to live** in London. |
| People **thought** that she knew the thief. | **It was thought that** she knew the thief. | She **was thought to know** the thief. |
| People **say** that he escaped by boat. | **It is said that** he escaped by boat. | He **is said to have escaped** by boat. |

*Earlier action* → **to have + past participle**: *He is said **to have escaped**.*

### Passive infinitive and -ing form
- after verbs like *want, need, hope, have to*: **to be + past participle** – *The cameras **need to be checked**.* · *She doesn’t want **to be seen**.*
- after verbs like *like, hate, avoid* and after prepositions: **being + past participle** – *He hates **being told** what to do.* · *The thief escaped without **being seen**.*

## 9 · Spoken English: got instead of was {#get-passive}

When we **talk**, we **can** also use **get / got** instead of **be / was**. It sounds more casual. People often use it when something happens **suddenly** or it is **bad news**.
*Beim Sprechen kann man statt „was“ auch „got“ sagen – das klingt lockerer. Man muss es aber nicht: „was“ ist immer richtig.*

<PassiveGraphic
  name="got"
  caption="got + 3rd form. With get you need do / did in questions and negatives. Never use was and got together."
/>

| be-passive (always correct) | get-passive (casual, spoken) |
|---|---|
| The thief **was caught**. | The thief **got caught**. |
| My phone **was stolen**. | My phone **got stolen**. |
| The window **was broken**. | The window **got broken**. |
| Nobody **was hurt**. | Nobody **got hurt**. |
| **Was** the thief caught? | **Did** the thief **get** caught? |
| He **wasn’t** caught. | He **didn’t get** caught. |

::: danger The 4 biggest “got” mistakes
1. ✗ Biscuit **got catch**. → ✓ Biscuit **got caught**. (got + **3rd form**)
2. ✗ Biscuit **was got** caught. → ✓ Biscuit **was** caught. / Biscuit **got** caught. (**be or got** – never both)
3. ✗ **Got** Biscuit caught? · ✗ He **gotn’t** caught. → ✓ **Did** Biscuit **get** caught? · ✓ He **didn’t get** caught. (get needs **do / did**)
4. ✗ I **got fixed my bike**. → ✓ I **got my bike fixed**. (get + **thing** + 3rd form)
:::

::: tip got or was?
📝 **was / were** is **always correct** – when you write **and** when you talk.
🗣️ **got** is an **extra, casual** way to say it when you talk. You never *have to* use it.
In a **police report**, a **text** or a **test**, use **was / were**.
:::

## Everything at a glance {#everything-at-a-glance}

| | active | passive |
|---|---|---|
| **present simple** | They **check** the alarm every day. | The alarm **is checked** every day. |
| **past simple** | Someone **stole** the painting. | The painting **was stolen**. |
| **present perfect** | They **have found** the car. | The car **has been found**. |
| **progressive** | They **are searching** the house. | The house **is being searched**. |
| **future** | They **will catch** the thief. | The thief **will be caught**. |
| **modal verb** | You **must** lock the door. | The door **must be locked**. |
| **by-agent** | **A camera** filmed the thief. | The thief was filmed **by a camera**. |
| **question** | **Did** they find the bag? | **Was** the bag **found**? |
| **two objects** | Someone gave **me** a key. | **I was given** a key. |
| **have sth done** | A builder repaired her roof. | She **had** her roof **repaired**. |
| **personal passive** | People say he is rich. | He **is said to be** rich. |
| **spoken: got** | Someone stole my phone. | My phone **got stolen**. |

::: tip Step by step: from active to passive
1. Find the **object** → it becomes the **new subject** at the front
2. Find the **tense** of the active verb
3. Put **be** into that tense – it must agree with the new subject (*was* / *were*)
4. Add the **past participle** (3rd form)
5. **by …**? Only if it is important who did it
:::

## Check yourself {#check-yourself}

Put the sentences into the passive. Then open the solution.

1. Someone **stole** my bike yesterday. → My bike …
2. The police **check** the cameras every night. → The cameras …
3. They **are questioning** the witnesses now. → The witnesses …
4. Someone **has broken** two windows. → Two windows …
5. You **must lock** the door. → The door …
6. **Did** they **find** the money? → … the money …?
7. Someone **gave** the guard a note. → The guard …
8. People **say** that the thief **is** very clever. → The thief …
9. *(spoken, with got)* The police **caught** the thief. → The thief …
10. *(spoken, question with got)* Did they catch the thief? → … the thief … caught?

::: details Solutions
1. My bike **was stolen** yesterday.
2. The cameras **are checked** every night.
3. The witnesses **are being questioned** now.
4. Two windows **have been broken**.
5. The door **must be locked**.
6. **Was** the money **found**?
7. The guard **was given** a note.
8. The thief **is said to be** very clever. (or: It is said that the thief is very clever.)
9. The thief **got caught**.
10. **Did** the thief **get** caught?
:::
