# 3 · Frequency adverbs & the middle position <Badge type="tip" text="class 5–6" />

## Goals
- put **always, usually, often, sometimes, never** in the right place
- know the three rules: **before the main verb – after *be* – after the first helping verb**
- put **every day, twice a week …** at the **end**
- use **also, just, already, even, still** in the middle position, too

Small words like *always* or *never* don’t go at the end and don’t go at the start – they go in the **middle** of the sentence.
*Im Deutschen steht „immer“ nach dem Verb (Sie kommt immer zu spät). Im Englischen steht es meistens vor dem Vollverb: She always comes late.*

## The three rules {#rules}

<WordOrderGraphic
  name="mid"
  caption="Before the main verb · after am / is / are · after the first helping verb. Longer time phrases go at the end."
/>

| rule | ✓ example | Deutsch |
|---|---|---|
| **before the main verb** | She **always gets up** early. | Sie steht immer früh auf. |
| | We **often play** cards. | Wir spielen oft Karten. |
| **after *be*** (am, is, are, was, were) | She **is always** late. | Sie ist immer zu spät. |
| | They **were never** at home. | Sie waren nie zu Hause. |
| **after the first helping verb** | I **have never been** to Paris. | Ich war noch nie in Paris. |
| | You **can always call** me. | Du kannst mich immer anrufen. |
| | He **doesn’t often eat** meat. | Er isst nicht oft Fleisch. |

::: tip The middle position words
**always – usually – normally – often – sometimes – rarely – never**
and also: **already, just, still, also, even, almost, really**
- I’ve **just** finished. – She has **already** left. – We **also** have a dog. – He **even** knows the teacher.
:::

## Questions and negatives {#questions}

In questions, the adverb comes **after the subject**:
- **Do** you **often go** swimming?
- **Is** she **always** so quiet?
- **Have** you **ever been** to London?

In negatives, it usually comes **after not**:
- I **don’t usually** get up so early.
- She **isn’t always** nice.

But *sometimes* goes before *don’t*: *I **sometimes don’t** have breakfast.*

## At the start or at the end? {#start-end}

- **sometimes, usually, normally, often** can also go **at the start** – for emphasis:
  ***Sometimes** I walk to school.*
- **always, never, already, just** – (almost) **never** at the start.
- **Longer phrases** go at the **end**: *every day, twice a week, on Mondays, once a month, from time to time*:
  *She gets up early **every day**.* – ✗ *She every day gets up early.*

## also or too? {#also-too}

| word | position | example |
|---|---|---|
| **also** | middle | I **also** like jazz. · She is **also** from Berlin. |
| **too** | end | I like jazz, **too**. · Me **too**! |
| **as well** | end | I like jazz **as well**. |

## Typical mistakes {#mistakes}

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ *She gets up always early.* → ✓ She **always gets up** early.
✗ *He always is late.* → ✓ He **is always** late.
✗ *I never have seen a whale.* → ✓ I **have never seen** a whale.
✗ *Always I am tired.* → ✓ **I am always** tired.
✗ *We every day go to school.* → ✓ We go to school **every day**.
✗ *I like also football.* → ✓ I **also like** football.
:::

## Check yourself {#check-yourself}

Put the adverb in the right place.

1. Tom is hungry. (always)
2. We go to the cinema. (often)
3. I have eaten sushi. (never)
4. Do you walk to school? (usually)
5. She helps her mum. (every day)
6. They can find a parking space. (never)

::: details Solutions
1. Tom **is always** hungry. (after *be*)
2. We **often go** to the cinema. (before the main verb)
3. I **have never eaten** sushi. (after the first helping verb)
4. Do you **usually walk** to school? (after the subject in questions)
5. She helps her mum **every day**. (long phrase → end)
6. They **can never find** a parking space. (after the helping verb *can*)
:::
