# 2 · Manner, place, time <Badge type="tip" text="class 5–6" />

## Goals
- put **how**, **where** and **when** in the right order: **M – P – T**
- know that time can also go to the **start** of the sentence
- avoid the German order *time before place*
- say *go **home***, *come **here*** – without *to*

After S – V – O come the extra information wagons: **how?**, **where?** and **when?**
The rule is easy: **manner – place – time**. In German it’s often the other way round.
*Im Deutschen sagt man oft erst die Zeit, dann den Ort: „Ich bin heute in der Schule.“ Im Englischen: Ort vor Zeit – I’m **at school today**.*

| S – V – O | M · how? | P · where? | T · when? |
|---|---|---|---|
| She sings | **beautifully** | **at the concert** | **tonight**. |
| I went | **by bus** | **to school** | **yesterday**. |
| We played | **really well** | **in the final** | **last week**. |
| Tom is | | **at home** | **now**. |
| My parents met | | **in London** | **in 2005**. |

::: tip Memory trick: “Pizza Tonight”
**P**lace before **T**ime – just like a **P**izza **T**onight. 🍕
*Oder alphabetisch: P kommt vor T.*
:::

You don’t need all three. But the ones you use stay in this order: *I was **at school** **on Monday**.*

## Time at the start {#time-first}

If you want to stress the time, you can put it **at the start** – often with a comma. Then the sentence goes on with **S – V** as usual ([→ chapter 1](./basics#time-first)):

- **On Saturday**, we played in the park.
- **Last summer**, my family went to Spain.
- **Tomorrow** I’m meeting Anna at the station.

::: warning Not in the middle!
Time phrases like *yesterday, last week, on Monday* don’t go between the verb and the object:
✗ *I saw yesterday Tom.* → ✓ I saw **Tom yesterday**. / **Yesterday** I saw Tom.
:::

## Places: going somewhere {#movement}

With verbs of movement (*go, come, walk, drive, fly …*) the **place** comes **straight after the verb** – before anything else:

- I went **to the cinema** with Anna on Friday.
- She drove **to work** early this morning.

**home, here, there, abroad, downtown** don’t need *to*:

| ✗ | ✓ |
|---|---|
| *I go to home.* | I go **home**. |
| *Come to here!* | Come **here**! |
| *We went to there.* | We went **there**. |

But: *I’m **at home*** (where?) – *I’m going **home*** (where to?).

## Two times or two places {#small-big}

If you have **two times** or **two places**, the **smaller** one usually comes first:

- at **8 o’clock** on **Monday** morning
- on **Friday**, **5 May**
- in a small **hotel** in **Rome**
- at number **12**, **Baker Street**, **London**

*Im Deutschen oft umgekehrt: am Montag um 8 Uhr – im Englischen eher: at 8 o’clock on Monday.*

## Typical mistakes {#mistakes}

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ *I was yesterday at the cinema.* → ✓ I was **at the cinema yesterday**.
✗ *We meet on Sunday at the park.* → ✓ We meet **at the park on Sunday**.
✗ *She plays every day piano.* → ✓ She plays **the piano every day**.
✗ *Let’s go to home.* → ✓ Let’s go **home**.
✗ *He speaks fluently English.* → ✓ He speaks **English fluently**.
:::

## Check yourself {#check-yourself}

Put the words in the right order.

1. I / last night / at Tom’s house / was
2. in the lake / we / every summer / swim
3. carefully / Mia / the letter / read
4. home / after school / went / Ben
5. at 7 o’clock / on Saturday / I / got up
6. on Monday / at the station / I’ll meet you

::: details Solutions
1. **I was at Tom’s house last night.** (place before time)
2. **We swim in the lake every summer.**
3. **Mia read the letter carefully.** (verb + object, then manner)
4. **Ben went home after school.** (no *to* before *home*)
5. **I got up at 7 o’clock on Saturday.** (small before big)
6. **I’ll meet you at the station on Monday.** (or: On Monday, I’ll meet you at the station.)
:::
