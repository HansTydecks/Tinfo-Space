# 6 · Subordinate clauses & indirect questions <Badge type="tip" text="class 7–8" />

## Goals
- keep **S – V** in clauses with *because, when, if, that, although …*
- put the clause at the **start** or at the **end** of the sentence
- build **indirect questions** without question order: *where the station **is***
- build **relative clauses** with *who, which, that*

This is the **number one** mistake of German speakers: in German, the verb goes to the **end** after *weil, dass, wenn, ob*. In English, it **doesn’t**. A subordinate clause has the same order as a normal sentence.
*Nach because, that, when, if … bleibt die Satzstellung S – V – O. Das Verb wandert nie ans Ende.*

## No verb at the end! {#svo}

<WordOrderGraphic
  name="clauses"
  caption="German: weil ich müde war – verb at the end. English: because I was tired – S – V, like a main clause. Indirect questions use normal order, too."
/>

| conjunction | ✗ German order | ✓ English |
|---|---|---|
| **because** | *… because I tired was.* | … because **I was** tired. |
| **that** | *I think that he right is.* | I think (that) **he is** right. |
| **when** | *When I home came, …* | When **I came** home, … |
| **if** | *If you time have, …* | If **you have** time, … |
| **although** | *… although it cold was.* | … although **it was** cold. |
| **before / after** | *… after we the film seen had.* | … after **we had seen** the film. |

## Clause at the start or at the end {#position}

The subordinate clause can come **first** or **second**. If it comes first, use a **comma** – and the main clause starts with **S – V** as usual:

| clause first | clause second |
|---|---|
| **Because I was tired**, I went home. | I went home **because I was tired**. |
| **When it rains**, we stay inside. | We stay inside **when it rains**. |
| **If you need help**, call me. | Call me **if you need help**. |

::: warning No German verb jump in the main clause!
German: *Weil ich müde war, **ging ich** nach Hause.* (verb first)
✗ *Because I was tired, went I home.* → ✓ Because I was tired, **I went** home.
:::

## Indirect questions {#indirect-questions}

An **indirect question** is a question inside a sentence: *Do you know …? Can you tell me …? I wonder … I don’t know …*
After the question word comes **normal order: S – V**. No *do*, no question order.

| direct question | indirect question |
|---|---|
| Where **is** the station? | Can you tell me where **the station is**? |
| What time **does** the film **start**? | Do you know what time **the film starts**? |
| Why **did** she **leave**? | I wonder why **she left**. |
| **Are** you coming? | I don’t know **if** (whether) **you are coming**. |

::: tip Polite questions
Indirect questions are very **polite**: *Excuse me, could you tell me **where the toilets are**?*
They also come up in **reported speech** → [Reported speech · questions](../reported-speech/#questions).
:::

## Relative clauses {#relative}

A **relative clause** gives more information about a person or a thing. Inside the relative clause: normal order again.

| who / which / that | example |
|---|---|
| **who** (people) | The man **who lives next door** is a pilot. |
| **which** (things) | The bike **which I bought** was cheap. |
| **that** (people and things) | The film **that we saw** was great. |
| **–** (no word, if it’s the object) | The film **we saw** was great. |
| **whose** (possession) | That’s the girl **whose dog bit me**. |

- The verb in the relative clause doesn’t go to the end: ✗ *the man who next door lives*
- Prepositions go to the **end**: *the friend **I** went to Spain **with*** (= mit dem ich …)

## Typical mistakes {#mistakes}

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ *I stayed at home, because I ill was.* → ✓ I stayed at home because **I was** ill.
✗ *I know that he a dog has.* → ✓ I know that **he has** a dog.
✗ *Do you know where is the bank?* → ✓ Do you know where **the bank is**?
✗ *Can you tell me what time does the bus leave?* → ✓ … what time **the bus leaves**?
✗ *When I home came, I ate something.* → ✓ When **I came** home, I ate something.
:::

## Check yourself {#check-yourself}

Correct or complete the sentences.

1. I was late because I the bus missed.
2. If it tomorrow rains, we will stay at home.
3. Do you know where (Tom / live)?
4. Can you tell me when (the train / leave)?
5. The girl who (sit) next to me is from Italy.
6. I wonder if (she / be) at home.

::: details Solutions
1. I was late because **I missed the bus**.
2. If **it rains tomorrow**, we’ll stay at home.
3. Do you know where **Tom lives**?
4. Can you tell me when **the train leaves**?
5. The girl who **sits** next to me is from Italy.
6. I wonder if **she is** at home.
:::
