# 4 · Questions & negatives <Badge type="tip" text="class 5–7" />

## Goals
- build questions with the formula **QW – A – S – V**
- use **do / does / did** when there is no other helping verb
- know when you **don’t** need *do*: *be*, *can*, *will* … and **subject questions**
- put **prepositions** at the end: *Who are you talking **to**?*
- make **negatives** and **short answers**

In German, you just put the verb first: *Spielst du Fußball?* In English, you can’t: ✗ *Play you football?*
English questions need a **helping verb** in front of the subject.
*Im Englischen springt nie das Vollverb nach vorne, sondern immer ein Hilfsverb (do, be, have, can …).*

## The question formula {#formula}

<WordOrderGraphic
  name="question"
  caption="QW · A · S · V: question word – helping verb – subject – verb. No helping verb? Then use do / does / did. Who as subject: no do."
/>

| QW | A – helping verb | S – subject | V – verb | rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | **Do** | you | **like** | pizza? |
| **Where** | **does** | Tom | **live**? | |
| **What** | **did** | you | **do** | yesterday? |
| **Why** | **are** | they | **laughing**? | |
| **How long** | **have** | you | **known** | her? |
| **When** | **can** | we | **meet**? | |

::: tip Which helping verb?
- **be**, **have** (+ 3rd form), **can, will, must, should …** are already there → put them in front: ***Is** she …? **Have** you seen …? **Can** I …?*
- no helping verb in the statement? → add **do / does / did**: *You like pizza. → **Do** you like pizza?*
- after **does** and **did**, the verb has **no -s** and **no -ed**: ✗ *Does she plays?* ✗ *Did you went?*
:::

## Questions with be {#be}

*be* as the **main verb** doesn’t need *do*:

| ✓ | ✗ |
|---|---|
| **Are** you tired? | ✗ *Do you be tired?* |
| Where **is** my phone? | ✗ *Where does my phone be?* |
| **Was** it fun? | ✗ *Did it be fun?* |

## Who …? What …? Subject or object? {#subject-questions}

This is the trickiest part. Ask yourself: **is the question word the subject?**

| statement | ask for | question | do? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Somebody** called you. | the subject | **Who called** you? | ❌ no *do* – normal order |
| You called **somebody**. | the object | **Who did** you **call**? | ✅ *did* + verb |
| **Something** happened. | the subject | **What happened**? | ❌ ✗ *What did happen?* |
| You saw **something**. | the object | **What did** you **see**? | ✅ |
| **Which team** won? | the subject | **Which team won**? | ❌ |

*Wenn das Fragewort selbst das Subjekt ist, bleibt der Satz wie eine Aussage – ohne do.*

## Prepositions at the end {#prepositions}

In normal English, the preposition goes to the **end** of the question:

- **Who** are you talking **to**? (Mit wem sprichst du?)
- **What** are you looking **at**?
- **Where** do you come **from**?
- **What** is this book **about**?

*To whom are you talking?* is correct, too – but very formal.

## Negatives {#negatives}

Same idea: **not** goes after the helping verb. No helping verb? → **don’t / doesn’t / didn’t** + verb.

| statement | negative |
|---|---|
| I like spiders. | I **don’t like** spiders. |
| She plays tennis. | She **doesn’t play** tennis. |
| We went out. | We **didn’t go** out. |
| He is at home. | He **isn’t** at home. |
| They have finished. | They **haven’t finished**. |
| I can swim. | I **can’t** swim. |

::: warning Only one negative
✗ *I don’t know nothing.* → ✓ I **don’t** know **anything**. / I know **nothing**.
:::

## Short answers {#short-answers}

Answer with **the same helping verb** – don’t repeat the main verb:

| question | ✓ short answer | ✗ |
|---|---|---|
| **Do** you like pizza? | Yes, I **do**. / No, I **don’t**. | ✗ *Yes, I like.* |
| **Does** Tom play the guitar? | Yes, he **does**. / No, he **doesn’t**. | |
| **Is** she your sister? | Yes, she **is**. / No, she **isn’t**. | ✗ *Yes, she’s.* |
| **Have** you finished? | Yes, I **have**. / No, I **haven’t**. | |
| **Can** you swim? | Yes, I **can**. / No, I **can’t**. | |

## Question tags {#question-tags}

A **question tag** (*…, oder? / …, nicht wahr?*) also uses the helping verb – with the opposite sign:
- You **like** pizza, **don’t you**?
- She **isn’t** here, **is she**?
- They **have** finished, **haven’t they**?

## Typical mistakes {#mistakes}

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ *Play you football?* → ✓ **Do you play** football?
✗ *Where lives your aunt?* → ✓ Where **does your aunt live**?
✗ *Why you are sad?* → ✓ Why **are you** sad?
✗ *Who did win the game?* → ✓ **Who won** the game?
✗ *Did you saw the film?* → ✓ Did you **see** the film?
✗ *With who are you going?* → ✓ **Who** are you going **with**?
:::

## Check yourself {#check-yourself}

Make questions.

1. you / tennis / play? (every week)
2. where / your parents / work?
3. what / you / do / last weekend?
4. who / eat / my chocolate? (*somebody ate it*)
5. who / you / meet / at the party? (*you met somebody*)
6. what / you / talk / about?

::: details Solutions
1. **Do you play** tennis every week?
2. Where **do your parents work**?
3. What **did you do** last weekend?
4. **Who ate** my chocolate? – *who* is the subject → no *did*
5. Who **did you meet** at the party? – *who* is the object → *did*
6. What **are you talking about**? – preposition at the end
:::
