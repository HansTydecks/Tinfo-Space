# Word order <Badge type="tip" text="the big picture" />

## Goals
- understand **why** word order is so important in English
- know the **sentence train**: subject – verb – object – manner – place – time
- see where English and German are **different** – that’s where most mistakes happen
- remember the **5 golden rules**

English word order looks easy: *subject – verb – object*. But German speakers often bring their German word order into English – and then strange sentences come out: ✗ *Yesterday went I to the cinema.*
This page shows you the big picture. The chapters show you the details – and the typical traps.
*Im Deutschen darf vieles im Satz wandern – im Englischen kaum. Die Reihenfolge der Wörter trägt im Englischen die Bedeutung.*

## 1 · Why order matters so much in English {#why}

In German, the **endings** show who does what. So you can change the order:
*Der Hund beißt den Mann.* = *Den Mann beißt der Hund.* – it’s always the dog that bites.

English has (almost) **no endings**. Only the **position** shows who does what:

| sentence | meaning |
|---|---|
| The **dog** bites the **man**. | 🐕 bites 👨 |
| The **man** bites the **dog**. | 👨 bites 🐕 (!) |

::: tip That’s the whole secret
English is a language with a **fixed order**. The word order **is** the grammar.
*Was im Deutschen die Endungen (der/den) erledigen, erledigt im Englischen die Position.*
:::

## 2 · The sentence train {#train}

Think of every English sentence as a **train**. The wagons always come in the same order:

The colours are the same on every page of this chapter:

| colour | wagon | asks | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🟦 **blue** | **S**ubject | who? what? | **Tom**, **my sister**, **it** |
| 🟥 **pink** | **V**erb (also helping verbs: *do, have, will, can …*) | does what? | **plays**, **has seen**, **didn’t go** |
| 🟩 **green** | **O**bject | what? whom? | **football**, **her**, **a letter** |
| 🟧 **orange** | **M**anner · **P**lace · **T**ime | how? where? when? | **well** · **in the park** · **on Saturdays** |
| ⬜ **grey** | question words and linking words | | **where**, **because**, **that** |

## 3 · English vs German {#english-vs-german}

German has a **moving verb**: it stands in **2nd place** in a main clause, and at the **end** after *weil, dass, wenn …*.
English doesn’t do that. The order **S – V – O** stays the same – in every kind of statement.

  name="de-vs-en"
  caption="German: the verb wanders – 2nd place, after the time, or at the end. English: subject – verb – object, always."

## 4 · The 5 golden rules {#golden-rules}

::: tip The 5 golden rules
1. **Subject before verb** – also when the sentence starts with *Yesterday …* or *Today …* → [chapter 1](./basics)
2. **Verb and object stay together** – nothing between them: ✗ *I like very much pizza* → [chapter 1](./basics)
3. **Manner – place – time** at the end: *at school on Monday* → [chapter 2](./place-time)
4. **Questions** need a **helping verb** before the subject: ***Do** you like …? **Where does** she live?* → [chapter 4](./questions)
5. **Subordinate clauses** keep **S – V**: *because I **was** tired* – never the verb at the end → [chapter 6](./clauses)
:::

And two smaller rules:
- *always, often, never …* go in the **middle**: *She **always** gets up early.* → [chapter 3](./adverbs)
- With two objects or phrasal verbs, **pronouns** have a fixed place: *give **it** to him, turn **it** off* → [chapter 5](./objects)

Most English books in German schools (for example *Green Line* or *Access*) teach word order step by step.
Your book can be a little different – that’s OK.

## Everything at a glance {#everything-at-a-glance}

| what | order | ✓ example | ✗ typical mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| statement | S – V – O – M – P – T | Tom plays football well in the park on Saturdays. | ✗ Tom plays on Saturdays football. |
| time first | T, S – V – O | Yesterday, I went to the cinema. | ✗ Yesterday went I to the cinema. |
| *always, never* | S – adverb – V · be – adverb | She always gets up early. She is always late. | ✗ She gets up always early. |
| question | (QW) – A – S – V | Where does she live? | ✗ Where lives she? |
| subject question | Who – V | Who called you? | ✗ Who did call you? |
| negative | S – A + not – V | I don’t like spiders. | ✗ I like not spiders. |
| two objects | V – person – thing · V – it – to + person | Give me the book. Give it to me. | ✗ Explain me the rule. |
| phrasal verb + pronoun | V – it – particle | Turn it off. | ✗ Turn off it. |
| subordinate clause | conj. – S – V | … because I was tired. | ✗ … because I tired was. |
| indirect question | QW – S – V | Can you tell me where the station is? | ✗ … where is the station? |
