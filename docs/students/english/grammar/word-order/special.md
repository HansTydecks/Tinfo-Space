# 7 · Special word order: inversion & emphasis <Badge type="tip" text="class 9–11" />

## Goals
- answer with **So do I** and **Neither do I**
- understand **inversion** after *Never, Not only, Hardly, Only then …*
- know the formal *if*-clause without *if*: ***Had I known**, …*
- use **here / there** + verb: *Here **comes the bus**!*
- stress information with **cleft sentences**: *It was **Tom** who …*, *What I need is …*

You know the rule: English keeps **S – V**. But there are a few fixed patterns where English uses **question order** in a statement. This is called **inversion**.
*Inversion heißt: Hilfsverb vor Subjekt – wie in einer Frage. Das passiert aber nur in wenigen, festen Mustern – meistens in formellen Texten.*

## So do I · Neither do I {#so-neither}

To say *Ich auch / Ich auch nicht*, use **So / Neither + helping verb + subject**. The helping verb is the same as in the first sentence – or **do / does / did**:

| statement | me too | me neither |
|---|---|---|
| I **like** pizza. | **So do I**. | |
| I **don’t like** spiders. | | **Neither do I**. / **Nor do I**. |
| I**’m** tired. | **So am I**. | |
| I **can’t** swim. | | **Neither can I**. |
| Tom **went** to the party. | **So did Anna**. | |
| I **haven’t** finished. | | **Neither have I**. |

✗ *So I do.* ✗ *Me also.* – In spoken English, ***Me too*** and ***Me neither*** are fine, too.

## Inversion after negative words {#negative-adverbs}

When a sentence **starts** with a negative or limiting word, the next part has **question order** (helping verb – subject). This sounds **dramatic** or **formal** – you find it in stories, speeches and essays.

| normal | with inversion |
|---|---|
| I have **never** seen such a mess. | **Never have I** seen such a mess! |
| She **not only** sings, she also dances. | **Not only does she** sing, she also dances. |
| We had **hardly** arrived when it started to rain. | **Hardly had we** arrived when it started to rain. |
| I understood it **only then**. | **Only then did I** understand it. |
| You must **not** open this door **under any circumstances**. | **Under no circumstances must you** open this door. |
| I had **no sooner** sat down than the phone rang. | **No sooner had I** sat down than the phone rang. |

::: tip No helping verb? → do / does / did
Just like in a question: *She sings.* → *Not only **does she sing** …* – *I understood.* → *Only then **did I understand** …*
:::

## Conditionals without if {#conditional}

In formal English, you can leave out **if** and put the helping verb first:

| with if | without if (formal) |
|---|---|
| **If I had known**, I would have helped. | **Had I known**, I would have helped. |
| **If you should** need help, call us. | **Should you** need help, call us. |
| **If I were** you, I’d take the job. | **Were I** you, I’d take the job. |

## Here comes the bus! {#here-there}

After **here** and **there** at the start, a **noun** subject goes after the verb – but a **pronoun** doesn’t:

| noun | pronoun |
|---|---|
| **Here comes the bus**! | **Here it comes**! |
| **There goes my money**! | **There it goes**! |
| **Here is your coffee**. | **Here you are**. |

The same happens in stories after place phrases: *On the hill **stood an old castle**.* (formal / literary)

## Emphasis: cleft sentences {#cleft}

To **stress** one part of the sentence, you can split it (*cleft* = split):

| normal | stressed | Deutsch |
|---|---|---|
| Tom broke the window. | **It was Tom who** broke the window. | Tom war es, der … |
| I met her in Paris. | **It was in Paris that** I met her. | In Paris war es, wo … |
| I need a holiday. | **What I need is** a holiday. | Was ich brauche, ist … |
| She hates the noise. | **What she hates is** the noise. | |

*In German, you simply put the stressed part first: **Tom** hat das Fenster kaputt gemacht. English uses **It was … who / that** instead – because the order is fixed.*
In the passive chapter you find another way to move information: [It is said that … / He is said to …](../passive/).

## Typical mistakes {#mistakes}

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ *“I like jazz.” – “So I do.”* → ✓ **So do I**.
✗ *“I can’t dance.” – “Me too.”* → ✓ **Neither can I.** / **Me neither.**
✗ *Never I have seen this.* → ✓ **Never have I** seen this.
✗ *Not only she sings, …* → ✓ **Not only does she** sing, …
✗ *Here comes it!* → ✓ **Here it comes**!
:::

## Check yourself {#check-yourself}

React or rewrite.

1. “I love this song.” – (me too)
2. “I didn’t do my homework.” – (me neither)
3. I have never been so happy. → *Never …*
4. He not only lied, he also stole money. → *Not only …*
5. If I had seen him, I would have told him. → *Had …*
6. Mia found the key. → *It was …*

::: details Solutions
1. **So do I.**
2. **Neither did I.** (*did* – because of *didn’t*)
3. **Never have I been** so happy.
4. **Not only did he lie**, he also stole money.
5. **Had I seen him**, I would have told him.
6. **It was Mia who found** the key.
:::
