# 5 · Two objects & phrasal verbs <Badge type="tip" text="class 6–7" />

## Goals
- use verbs with **two objects**: *give **him** **the book*** / *give **the book** **to him***
- put **pronouns** (*it, them*) in the right place
- avoid the trap ✗ *explain me* → ✓ *explain **to** me*
- use **phrasal verbs** with nouns and pronouns: *turn **it** off*

Some verbs have **two** object wagons: a **thing** and a **person**. And some verbs have a little extra word (*off, up, on …*) that can move.
*Bei „geben, schicken, zeigen …“ gibt es zwei Möglichkeiten. Bei Pronomen und Phrasal Verbs gibt es feste Regeln.*

## Two objects: two ways {#two-objects}

| verb – **person** – **thing** | verb – **thing** – **to / for + person** |
|---|---|
| Give **Tom** **the book**. | Give **the book** **to Tom**. |
| I sent **my aunt** **a postcard**. | I sent **a postcard** **to my aunt**. |
| She showed **us** **her photos**. | She showed **her photos** **to us**. |
| Can you buy **me** **a drink**? | Can you buy **a drink** **for me**? |
| Mum made **us** **a cake**. | Mum made **a cake** **for us**. |

Both are correct. The **new or important** information usually goes **at the end**:
- *Who did you give the book to?* – *I gave the book **to Tom**.*
- *What did you give Tom?* – *I gave Tom **the book**.*

::: tip to or for?
- **to** = the thing goes **to** somebody: *give, send, show, bring, lend, tell, pass, sell, write*
- **for** = you do it **for** somebody: *buy, make, get, cook, find, book*
:::

## Pronouns: it and them {#pronouns}

If the **thing** is a pronoun (*it, them*), use the **to / for** pattern:

| ✓ | ✗ |
|---|---|
| Give **it to me**. | ✗ *Give me it.* |
| I sent **them to my aunt**. | ✗ *I sent my aunt them.* |
| Can you show **it to us**? | ✗ *Can you show us it?* |

But if only the **person** is a pronoun, both ways are fine: *Give **me** the book.* / *Give the book **to me**.*

## Watch out: explain, describe, say … {#explain}

Some verbs **can’t** have the person directly after them. They always need **to**:

| ✗ (German order: „erklär mir …“) | ✓ |
|---|---|
| *Can you explain me the rule?* | Can you explain **the rule to me**? |
| *Describe me your house.* | Describe **your house to me**. |
| *She said me the answer.* | She **told me** the answer. / She **said** it **to me**. |
| *I suggested him a film.* | I suggested **a film to him**. |

Verbs like this: **explain, describe, say, suggest, report, introduce, mention**.
*Merke: explain/describe/say + Sache + **to** + Person. Bei „tell“ steht die Person direkt dahinter: tell **me** a story.*

## Phrasal verbs {#phrasal-verbs}

A **phrasal verb** is a verb + a little word: *turn **off**, pick **up**, put **on**, throw **away**, give **back**, fill **in** …*

| object | position | example |
|---|---|---|
| **noun** | in the middle **or** at the end | Turn **the TV** off. = Turn off **the TV**. |
| | | Pick **your bag** up. = Pick up **your bag**. |
| **pronoun** (*it, them, him, her …*) | **only in the middle** | Turn **it** off. – ✗ *Turn off it.* |
| | | Pick **them** up. – ✗ *Pick up them.* |
| | | Put **it** on. – ✗ *Put on it.* |

::: warning Not all phrasal verbs can be split
With verbs like *look **after**, look **for**, get **on** (the bus), listen **to***, the object always comes **after** the little word:
*I’m looking **for** my keys.* – *I’m looking **for them**.* – ✗ *I’m looking them for.*
Your dictionary shows it: *turn sth off* (can split) – *look after sb* (can’t split).
:::

## Typical mistakes {#mistakes}

::: danger Typical mistakes
✗ *Explain me the homework, please.* → ✓ Explain **the homework to me**, please.
✗ *Give me it!* → ✓ Give **it to me**!
✗ *Switch off it, please.* → ✓ Switch **it off**, please.
✗ *Can you pick up me at 6?* → ✓ Can you pick **me up** at 6?
:::

## Check yourself {#check-yourself}

Put the words in the right order.

1. sent / I / a postcard / my grandma (two ways)
2. it / give / to me
3. the rules / to us / explained / the teacher
4. off / it / turn / please
5. up / them / pick / can you?
6. a cake / for my birthday / made / me / my dad

::: details Solutions
1. **I sent my grandma a postcard.** / **I sent a postcard to my grandma.**
2. **Give it to me.** – ✗ *Give me it*
3. **The teacher explained the rules to us.** – *explain* always with *to*
4. **Turn it off, please.** – pronoun in the middle
5. **Can you pick them up?**
6. **My dad made me a cake for my birthday.**
:::
